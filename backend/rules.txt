Rule 3: Applicability of Chapter II

Chapter II does not apply to:

Packages containing more than 25 kg or 25 litre
Cement, fertilizer and agricultural farm produce sold in bags above 50 kg
Packages meant for industrial or institutional consumers.

Rule 4: Regulation for pre-packing and sale

A commodity intended for sale/distribution/delivery must carry the declarations required under the rules.

An interesting exception:

Packages without MRP inside the manufacturer's premises aren't automatically a violation, provided packages leaving the premises carry the required MRP. Promotional groups also have declaration requirements.

Rule 6: Declarations on EVERY package

specifies the declarations that must appear on packages.

The major fields include:

6(1)(a): Manufacturer / packer / importer

Depending on the package:

Manufacturer name/address
Packer name/address where different
Importer name/address for imported packages

There are special provisions for electronic products involving QR codes.

The rule also contains important responsibility presumptions regarding names/addresses and brand owners.

6(1)(aa): Country of origin

Imported products must declare:

Country of origin / manufacture / assembly.

6(1)(b): Common/generic name

The common/generic name of the commodity must be declared.

For multiple products, the package must identify each product and its number/quantity. Electronic products have QR-related provisions.

6(1)(c): Net quantity

The package must declare net quantity in the appropriate standard unit, or number when sold by number.

6(1)(d): Month and year

Month and year of manufacture are required, subject to the exceptions and special provisions in the rule.

6(1)(da): Best before / use by

For commodities that may become unfit for human consumption after a period, best before/use by date must be declared unless another law provides for it.

6(1)(e): MRP

The retail sale price must be declared as the maximum retail price inclusive of all taxes, in Indian currency.

6(1)(f): Dimensions

Where relevant, package dimensions of the commodity must be declared, including dimensions of different pieces where necessary.

Electronic products have QR-code provisions for size/dimension information.

6(1)(g): Other prescribed information

This brings in additional special declarations and exceptions, including:

Certain exemptions for bidi/incense sticks
Specific LPG provisions
Packaging-material transition provisions
MRP exceptions for bidi and certain LPG cylinders.
Rule 6(2): Consumer complaints

Every package must bear:

Name
Address
Telephone number
E-mail address, if available

of the person/office that can be contacted for consumer complaints.

Electronic products have specific QR provisions.

Rule 12: Manner of quantity declaration

Quantity should be expressed using appropriate:

Mass
Length
Area
Volume
Number

depending on the commodity.

There are exceptions in the Fourth Schedule. Additional dimensions/number may also be required where necessary. The declaration cannot use wording that creates an exaggerated, misleading or inadequate impression of quantity.

System

      ↓

      ↓

      ↓
Compare

And detect suspicious wording such as:

"approximately"
"about"
"minimum"

where prohibited by the rule.

This defines how units should be expressed.

Examples:

<1 kg → grams
≥1 kg → kilograms
<1 m → centimetres
≥1 m → metres
<1 L → millilitres
≥1 L → litres

It also prohibits non-SI systems for net quantity and controls number/unit/piece terminology.

Where multiple identical packages are contained in one prepackage, the number and quantity of each package must also be indicated.

⭐ Rule 26: Exemptions

This is critical for avoiding false positives.

The rules contain exemptions for certain packages, including:

26(a)

Very small quantities:

10 g / 10 ml or less under the specified condition.

The tobacco-related exception also matters.

26(b)

Fast food items packed by restaurants/hotels and similar establishments.

26(c)

Specified pharmaceutical formulations covered by the Drugs (Price Control) Order, subject to the medical-device proviso.

26(d)

Agricultural farm produce provision was omitted in the 2017 amendment.

26(e)

Thread sold in coils to handloom weavers.

26(f)

Certain garments/hosiery sold loose or open where consumers can inspect them before buying.

However, specified information still needs to be provided, including:

Manufacturer/marketer/brand owner/importer
Country of origin for imported products
Consumer-care contact
Size information
MRP

and e-commerce display where applicable.

26(g)

Certain loose commodities ordered through e-commerce channels, with specified information including:

Manufacturer/marketer/brand owner/importer/seller
Country of origin where applicable
Consumer care
Retail sale price
Net quantity.
